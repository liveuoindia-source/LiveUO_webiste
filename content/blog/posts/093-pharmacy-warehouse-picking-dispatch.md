In a pharmacy warehouse, a picking error doesn't just annoy a customer. The wrong strength, the wrong batch or a short-expiry pack can reach a branch or a retailer — and eventually a patient. Accurate picking and dispatch is as much about safety as efficiency.

## Organise the warehouse first

- **Bin locations** for every item — rack, shelf, position — recorded in the system.
- **Look-alike and sound-alike medicines** kept apart, with clear labels.
- **Fast movers near dispatch.**
- **Separate areas** for cold-chain, scheduled drugs, returns and expired stock.

## Pick lists from the system

Each order or indent generates a pick list showing:

- item, strength and pack
- quantity
- **bin location**, in walking order
- **batch to pick**, chosen by FEFO
- expiry

Pickers follow the list. They shouldn't choose batches themselves.

## FEFO: first expiry, first out

The system should allocate the batch with the earliest expiry that still meets the minimum shelf life required for that customer or branch. This keeps warehouse stock fresh and prevents short-expiry stock from being sent where it won't sell.

## Scan to confirm

Barcode scanning at picking catches most errors: wrong item, wrong strength, wrong batch. Where barcodes aren't available, a second person checks against the pick list.

## Check before packing

A separate check — by a different person or by scanning again — before packing:

- item and strength
- quantity
- batch and expiry
- condition

## Pack properly

- cold-chain items in proper cold packs, packed last
- fragile items protected
- packing list included
- cartons labelled with destination and order number

## Dispatch documents

Generate from the same system:

- transfer note or delivery challan for own branches, or tax invoice for sales, as appropriate
- e-way bill where the value and movement require it
- route or delivery sheet

## Receipt confirmation

Branches or customers confirm receipt against the dispatch. Shortages or wrong items are reported immediately and traced back to the pick list. See [inter-branch stock transfers](/resources/blog/inter-branch-stock-transfer-pharmacy/).

## Measure accuracy

Track:

- picking errors per hundred lines
- errors by type — wrong item, wrong quantity, wrong batch
- errors by picker and shift
- short-expiry dispatches
- dispatch time from order to loading

Review weekly and address patterns — a confusing bin location, a look-alike pair, a training gap.

## Common causes, and fixes

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Look-alike packs stored side by side | Separate them; add shelf warnings |
| Picker chooses an easier-to-reach batch | System allocates batch; scan to confirm |
| Bin locations out of date | Update locations when stock is moved; spot-check weekly |
| Rushed end-of-day dispatch | Set order cut-off times; stagger picking |
| Partial packs mixed with full packs | Separate loose stock clearly |
| New staff unfamiliar with the range | Pair with experienced pickers; start on simple orders |

## Returns into the warehouse

Stock coming back — from branches, retailers or failed deliveries — needs its own process, or it contaminates good stock:

- receive returns in a separate area
- check item, batch, expiry and condition
- put saleable stock back to its correct bin, with the batch recorded
- move damaged, short-expiry or expired stock to a non-saleable location for supplier return or disposal

Returns put back without checks are one of the most common reasons a bin holds a batch the system doesn't know about.

## Training and accountability

Record who picked and who checked each order. When errors happen, it's then possible to see whether they cluster around a person, a shift or an item — and fix the real cause rather than blaming the whole team.

## The goal

A warehouse that picks the right item, from the right batch, in the right quantity, every time — and can prove it. With system-driven pick lists, FEFO allocation and scanning, that becomes the norm rather than the exception.
