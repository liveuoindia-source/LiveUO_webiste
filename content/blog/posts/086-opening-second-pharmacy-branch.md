The first pharmacy is run by the owner's presence: they see the counter, the shelves and the cash every day. The second branch breaks that model. The owner can't be in two places, and the habits that worked in one store — buying by eye, informal discounts, cash checked by feel — stop working.

The systems you put in place before opening the second store decide whether expansion doubles profit or doubles problems.

## Regulatory groundwork

- **Drug licence** for the new premises, with a registered pharmacist.
- **GST** — add the new premises as an additional place of business, or register separately if in another state.
- **Shop and establishment registration** and other local requirements.

Start these early; approvals take time.

## One system, two branches

Run both branches on the same software:

- **shared item master** — same item names, categories and HSN codes
- **branch-wise stock**, visible from either location
- **inter-branch transfers** with batch and expiry
- **branch-wise and combined reports**

With separate systems, every comparison and transfer becomes manual.

## Set up user roles

The second branch will often run without you. Give each person their own login, and set permissions for discounts, cancellations, stock adjustments and price changes. See [user roles in pharmacy software](/resources/blog/pharmacy-user-roles-permissions/).

## Opening stock

- decide the initial range based on the new location's likely customers
- load opening stock with batch, expiry and cost
- consider transferring some stock from the first branch rather than buying everything new

Don't simply copy the first store's range. Different neighbourhoods buy differently.

## Purchasing

Decide early:

- will each branch buy separately, or will you buy centrally?
- which distributors will serve the new branch?
- will rates be the same at both?

Central purchasing becomes more valuable as you grow — see [centralised purchasing](/resources/blog/centralised-purchasing-pharmacy-chain/).

## Cash and daily closing

Set a standard closing routine at both branches, with day-end reports sent to you automatically. See [day-end cash reconciliation](/resources/blog/pharmacy-day-end-cash-reconciliation/).

## Reports you'll check daily

- sales by branch and payment mode
- discounts and cancellations
- stock-outs of key items
- near-expiry stock

Receive them automatically, so checking takes minutes.

## The first three months

- visit regularly, but rely on the system for daily oversight
- compare new branch sales with your expectations weekly
- adjust the range based on actual demand
- watch for slow-moving stock early

## Common mistakes

- running the new branch on separate software or paper
- shared logins
- copying the first store's range without checking local demand
- no daily reporting
- informal transfers between branches

A second branch is the moment a pharmacy becomes a small chain. Put the systems of a chain in place before opening, and every branch after it becomes easier.
