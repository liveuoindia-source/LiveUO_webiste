Many pharmacy losses begin at the back door, not the counter. A carton short by one strip, a batch that expires in eight weeks, an MRP that changed without notice, a scheme that was promised but not billed. None of these are dramatic. All of them are expensive when they're found weeks later, after the delivery boy has left and the invoice has been paid.

The fix is an inward check that happens every time, at the moment of delivery.

## What to check on every delivery

### 1. Quantity

Count against the invoice, not against the order. Count strips or units, not boxes — a box with a missing strip looks full on the shelf.

### 2. Batch number

Confirm the batch on the pack matches the invoice. A batch mismatch breaks traceability: if that batch is recalled, your records will point to the wrong shelf.

### 3. Expiry date

Check expiry on every batch. Agree a **minimum remaining shelf life** with each distributor — for example, refusing stock with less than a set number of months left, adjusted for how fast the item sells. Short-expiry stock accepted today is tomorrow's write-off.

### 4. MRP

Compare the MRP printed on the pack with the invoice and with your last purchase. An MRP change affects selling price, margin and possibly existing stock. It should never slip in unnoticed.

### 5. Rate and discount

Check the purchase rate and trade discount against what was agreed. A rate increase of a few rupees per strip is easy to miss on a long invoice and adds up over a year.

### 6. Scheme and free quantity

If the distributor offered free goods or a scheme, confirm they're on the invoice and in the box. Schemes promised verbally and forgotten at billing are a common source of quiet margin loss.

### 7. Condition

Look for damaged packs, broken seals, and for cold-chain items, the condition of the cold pack or temperature indicator. Refuse what you wouldn't sell.

## Enter the purchase while the stock is in front of you

The most important habit: **do the purchase entry against the physical stock, not later from the paper invoice.**

When entry happens hours later from the invoice alone, the person entering it can't see the box. They copy what the distributor wrote, including its mistakes. When entry happens at receipt, every line is checked against what actually arrived.

Good pharmacy software makes this faster, not slower:

- the item, last purchase rate and last MRP appear as soon as the item is selected
- rate increases and MRP changes are highlighted
- expiry below your minimum shelf life triggers a warning
- free quantity is entered separately so cost per unit is calculated correctly

## Record differences immediately

When something doesn't match:

1. **Enter what you actually received**, not what was billed.
2. **Note the difference** on the delivery copy and get the delivery person to sign it, where possible.
3. **Inform the distributor the same day**, with photos for damage or short expiry.
4. **Track the claim** until the credit note arrives.

A short-supply or rate-difference claim raised the same day is usually settled. One raised next month usually isn't.

## A simple inward register

Keep a running record of every discrepancy:

| Date | Distributor | Invoice | Issue | Value | Claim status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04 Nov | ABC Pharma | 1188 | 1 strip short | ₹96 | Credit note received |
| 06 Nov | XYZ Distributors | 2207 | Rate +₹3/strip | ₹360 | Disputed |
| 09 Nov | ABC Pharma | 1241 | Expiry 2 months | ₹1,240 | Returned at door |

Over a few months, this shows which distributors are reliable — useful when you're deciding where to place larger orders.

## Who should do the check

Make one person responsible per shift, and let the system record who entered each purchase. Shared responsibility for receiving tends to mean nobody checks carefully when the counter is busy.

If the pharmacist is also running the counter, set a rule: deliveries are checked within an hour of arrival, before stock goes on the shelf. Stock that is shelved unchecked is very hard to verify afterwards.

## Connect it to supplier returns

Expiry-short or damaged stock that does get through should go straight into a supplier return, not onto the shelf. See [supplier returns and credit notes](/resources/blog/supplier-returns-credit-notes-pharmacy/) for how to track those through to the credit note.

The inward check takes minutes. It is one of the few controls that stops a loss before it happens, rather than explaining it afterwards.
