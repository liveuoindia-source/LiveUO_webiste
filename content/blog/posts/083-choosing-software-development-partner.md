Choosing a software development partner is a decision you'll live with for years — through the build, launch and every change after. The cheapest quote and the best sales presentation are rarely the best guides.

## Relevant experience

Look for partners who have built:

- software for businesses like yours, or with similar workflows
- systems of similar size and complexity
- the integrations you need — payments, WhatsApp, accounting, devices

Ask to see working software, not just screenshots. A demo of a real system tells you more than a portfolio page.

## How they understand your business

In early conversations, notice whether they:

- ask about your process, exceptions and users — not just features
- push back when something seems unclear or unnecessary
- suggest a smaller first release
- explain trade-offs in plain language

A partner who agrees to everything in the first meeting will be negotiating every change later.

## How they handle scope and change

Ask:

- How do you define scope?
- What happens when we want to change something mid-project?
- How do you estimate, and what assumptions do you make?
- What's included and excluded in your quote?

Good partners have a clear, written process for change requests.

## Communication

- Who is your main contact, and how often will you hear from them?
- Will you see working software regularly — every week or two?
- How are issues reported and tracked?
- Do they communicate clearly in the language your team uses?

## Quality and testing

- How do they test?
- Is there a testing or staging environment for you to try changes?
- How are bugs found after launch handled?
- What's the warranty period?

## Ownership and handover

Agree in writing:

- who owns the source code and deliverables
- how pre-existing components are licensed
- handover of code, credentials, documentation and hosting access
- what happens if the relationship ends

## Security and data

If the software handles personal or financial data, ask how they handle access control, encryption, backups and secure development. Make sure any data they access during development is protected.

## Support after launch

- support hours and response times
- cost of ongoing support and small changes
- hosting and monitoring
- how long they've supported past clients

## References

Speak to at least two past clients. Ask about delivery against promises, how problems were handled, and whether they'd hire them again.

## Start small

A short paid discovery phase or pilot is the best test. You'll see how they ask questions, document requirements, communicate and deliver — before committing to the full project.
