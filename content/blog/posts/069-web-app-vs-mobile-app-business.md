"We need an app" is one of the most common starting points for custom software. Often, what the business needs is software that works well on phones — which isn't the same thing. The choice between a web application and a native mobile app affects cost, timeline and maintenance for years.

## The options

**Responsive web application.** Runs in the browser on any device, adjusting to screen size. One codebase, updated instantly for everyone.

**Progressive web app (PWA).** A web application that can be installed on the home screen, loads fast, and works partly offline. No app store.

**Native mobile app.** Built for Android and/or iOS, installed from app stores, with full access to device features.

**Cross-platform app.** One codebase producing Android and iOS apps — less cost than two native apps, with most of the capability.

## When a web app is enough

- users are mostly in offices, or work on laptops and desktops
- the software is internal — staff, managers, partners
- the main tasks are data entry, approvals, reports and dashboards
- internet access is reasonably reliable
- you want updates to reach everyone instantly

Most internal business systems — order management, CRM, HR, reporting — fall here.

## When a native or cross-platform app is worth it

- **field staff** working with patchy connectivity, who need dependable offline work
- **heavy device use** — barcode scanning with the camera, GPS tracking, Bluetooth printers, photos
- **background tasks** — location tracking, sync while the app is closed
- **frequent customer use**, where an icon on the home screen and push notifications drive engagement
- **app store presence** is part of the brand or expected by customers

## Where PWAs fit

A PWA covers a lot of middle ground: installable, fast, partly offline, with access to some device features. It suits internal tools used on phones, simple customer portals, and businesses that want an "app" without app store overhead. Device support varies, so test on the phones your users actually have.

## Compare the costs

| | Web / PWA | Cross-platform | Native (both) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build cost | Lowest | Medium | Highest |
| Codebases | One | One | Two |
| Updates | Instant | App store release | App store release |
| Offline | Limited to good | Good | Best |
| Device features | Some | Most | All |
| Ongoing maintenance | Lowest | Medium | Highest |

Remember app maintenance: OS updates, store policy changes and device testing continue as long as the app exists.

## A practical approach

1. **Build the backend with APIs** from the start, whatever the front end.
2. **Launch with a responsive web app or PWA** for most users.
3. **Add a mobile app** for specific roles — field staff, delivery, customers — once the need is proven.

This keeps the first release smaller and avoids paying for app development before you know it's needed.

## Questions to answer before deciding

- Who are the users, and where do they work?
- How reliable is their internet?
- Which device features are truly needed?
- How often will they use it — daily, or occasionally?
- Is it internal or customer-facing?
- What's the budget for building and maintaining it?

The right answer depends on users and tasks, not on what sounds modern. Most businesses are best served by an excellent web application first, with mobile apps added where they clearly earn their cost.
