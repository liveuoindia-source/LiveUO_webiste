"How much will it cost?" is the first question about any custom software project, and the honest answer is always "it depends". That's frustrating, but it's true — and understanding *what* it depends on is the best way to get a reliable estimate and avoid surprises.

## The main cost drivers

### Scope and complexity

The number of screens, workflows, business rules, user roles and reports. Exceptions add more than the main path: partial payments, approvals, returns, multi-currency, multiple branches.

### Integrations

Each connection to another system — accounting, payment gateway, WhatsApp, courier, ERP, biometric devices — adds work. Well-documented APIs are faster; old systems without APIs are slower and riskier.

### Platforms

A web application costs less than a web app plus Android and iOS apps. See [web app or mobile app](/resources/blog/web-app-vs-mobile-app-business/) for the trade-offs.

### Design

Clean, standard interfaces cost less than highly customised visual design. For internal tools, usability matters far more than visual polish.

### Data migration

Moving data from spreadsheets or old systems — cleaning, mapping, testing — is often underestimated.

### Users and scale

Ten internal users is different from thousands of customers. Performance, security and hosting needs grow with scale.

### Compliance and security

Personal data, payments, healthcare or financial information need stronger security, audit trails and testing.

### Testing and quality

Thorough testing takes time. Quotes that look cheap often skimp here.

### Support after launch

Fixes, small changes, hosting, monitoring and updates continue for as long as the software is used.

## The cost components

| Component | Typical share of effort |
|---|---|
| Discovery and design | Smaller, but sets everything else |
| Development | Largest |
| Testing | Significant |
| Data migration | Varies widely |
| Deployment and training | Smaller |
| Ongoing support | Recurring |

## Getting comparable quotes

To compare vendors fairly:

1. **Share the same written scope** with each. See [how to write a requirements document](/resources/blog/software-requirements-document-guide/).
2. **Ask for a breakdown** by module or phase, not one number.
3. **Ask what's excluded** — migration, integrations, hosting, support, training.
4. **Ask about ownership** of code and handover.
5. **Ask about testing** and the warranty period.
6. **Check references** from similar projects.

## Ways to reduce cost without cutting corners

- **Smaller first release** focused on the core workflow — see [scoping a first release](/resources/blog/mvp-scope-business-software/).
- **Use proven components** for login, payments, notifications and reporting.
- **Clean your data** before migration.
- **Make decisions quickly.** Waiting on approvals extends timelines, and on time-and-material contracts, cost.
- **Involve users early** to avoid rework.

## Total cost of ownership

The build is only part of the cost. Over several years, include hosting, support, maintenance, updates and improvements. A realistic budget plans for all of them from the start.

## The bottom line

A good estimate comes from a clear scope, an honest list of assumptions, and a vendor who explains what's included and what isn't. Spend time on those, and the number you get will be one you can actually rely on.
