With one pharmacy, the owner knows the numbers by walking the floor. With five or ten, that stops working. The owner needs a single view of the whole chain — and the ability to see quickly which branch is doing well, which is struggling, and why.

## Consolidation needs a common foundation

Before reports can be combined, branches must share:

- **one item master** — same names, categories, manufacturers
- **one set of definitions** — what counts as a sale, how returns are treated, which cost is used for margin
- **one system**, or at least systems that sync to a central database

Without these, consolidated totals are sums of inconsistent numbers.

## The core chain reports

### Sales

- sales by branch, day, week and month
- growth versus the same period last year
- sales by category, manufacturer and top items
- average bill value and number of bills

### Margin

- gross margin by branch, in value and percentage
- margin by category
- discount given, as a percentage of sales

### Stock

- stock value by branch
- stock days — how many days of sales the stock represents
- near-expiry stock value by branch
- dead and slow-moving stock

### Purchases

- purchases by supplier and branch
- purchase rates versus contracted rates
- off-contract purchases

### Operations

- stock-audit differences
- cancellations and returns by branch
- stockouts of key items

## Make branches comparable

Raw totals favour big branches. Compare on ratios:

| Metric | Why it's fair |
|---|---|
| Sales growth % | Independent of size |
| Gross margin % | Shows pricing and buying discipline |
| Discount % | Shows counter discipline |
| Stock days | Shows buying efficiency |
| Near-expiry as % of stock | Shows expiry control |
| Audit difference % | Shows stock control |

A branch with lower sales but higher margin and lower expiry may be better run than a larger one.

## Design for action

- **Exception lists**: branches outside normal ranges, items out of stock at several branches, near-expiry items that could be transferred.
- **Drill-down**: from chain total to branch to category to item.
- **Scheduled summaries**: daily sales by branch by WhatsApp or email; weekly exceptions; monthly full review.

## Monthly review format

1. Chain sales, margin and growth.
2. Branch ranking on the key ratios.
3. Exceptions and their reasons.
4. Actions agreed last month, and their status.
5. Actions for next month.

Keep it short and consistent. The same format every month makes trends obvious.

## Access by role

- **Owner and head office** — all branches.
- **Area managers** — their branches.
- **Branch managers** — their own branch, plus perhaps a ranking.

## The payoff

Consolidated reporting turns a chain from a collection of shops into a business you can manage by numbers. Problems surface early, good practice at one branch can be copied to others, and decisions about purchasing, staffing and expansion rest on facts.
