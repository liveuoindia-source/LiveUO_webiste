Launching a direct-to-consumer brand is exciting — and easy to get wrong on details that customers notice immediately: slow pages, confusing checkout, missing policies, late deliveries. A checklist helps make sure the store is ready before the first customer arrives.

## Business and compliance

- business registration in place
- GST registration where required — confirm with your tax adviser
- product-specific licences or certifications, for example for food, cosmetics or electrical goods
- trademark search and application for your brand name
- legal metrology requirements on packaging — MRP, net quantity, manufacturer details, and so on, as applicable

## Website and store

- domain and professional email
- fast, mobile-first store — see [online store speed](/resources/blog/ecommerce-website-speed/)
- product pages with clear photos, specifics and size guides — see [anatomy of a product page that converts](/resources/blog/product-page-that-converts/)
- simple navigation and search
- guest checkout and PIN-code auto-fill

## Policies and trust

- privacy policy
- terms and conditions
- shipping policy with realistic timelines
- returns and refund policy
- contact details and a grievance contact
- secure checkout

Consumer protection rules for e-commerce require certain information to be displayed. Check what applies to your business.

## Payments

- payment gateway live, with UPI, cards and net banking — see [choosing a payment gateway](/resources/blog/payment-gateway-selection-india/)
- COD decision and rules — see [COD vs prepaid](/resources/blog/cod-vs-prepaid-orders-india/)
- refunds tested end to end

## Shipping and operations

- courier or aggregator account, with serviceable PIN codes checked
- packaging tested for damage in transit
- order processing routine — who packs, by when
- inventory recorded and synced
- returns process ready

## Communication

- order confirmation, shipping and delivery messages by email and WhatsApp
- support channel with response times
- FAQ page answering common questions

## Analytics

- analytics installed with e-commerce tracking
- conversion events for add-to-cart, checkout and purchase
- ad pixels configured, with consent where required
- a simple dashboard: sessions, conversion rate, orders, average order value

## Pre-launch test

Place real orders yourself:

- on a phone, over mobile data
- with UPI, card and COD
- to different PIN codes
- then return one

Fix everything that feels slow or confusing.

## First customers

- early-access list from your network and social media
- launch offer that doesn't destroy margin
- creators who genuinely fit the brand, with disclosed partnerships
- a small, targeted ad budget to learn which audiences respond

## After launch

Review weekly: traffic, conversion rate, RTO and returns, reviews, repeat purchases. Fix what customers struggle with before spending more on acquisition — see [turning first-time buyers into repeat customers](/resources/blog/ecommerce-repeat-purchase/).

A D2C launch isn't a single day. It's a few weeks of careful preparation, followed by months of learning from real customers. Getting the basics right at launch gives every later effort a better chance.
