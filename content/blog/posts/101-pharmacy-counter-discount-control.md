A pharmacy works on thin margins. A discount of a few percent given casually at the counter can remove a large share of the profit on that bill. Multiply that across hundreds of bills a day, and informal discounting becomes one of the biggest — and least visible — costs in the business.

## How discounts creep up

- a regular customer asks for "something extra" and gets it
- one staff member rounds down every bill
- discounts given to match an online price the customer mentions
- a festival offer that never formally ended
- different staff giving different discounts to the same customer

None of these is wrong on its own. Together, without a policy, they make margin unpredictable.

## Write a discount policy

A short written policy answers:

| Question | Example answer |
|---|---|
| Standard discount? | A fixed percentage on medicines; different on OTC and FMCG |
| Who gets more? | Loyalty members, senior citizens, institutional accounts |
| Maximum at counter? | Counter staff up to the standard; pharmacist a little higher |
| Above maximum? | Manager approval, with reason |
| Excluded items? | Low-margin items, certain categories |
| Rounding? | Round to nearest rupee, not down to tens |

Share it with all staff. Consistency is fairer for customers and easier for staff, who no longer have to decide case by case.

## Configure it in the software

- set default discounts by category
- set maximum discount by user role
- require a manager PIN and reason for exceptions
- apply customer-group discounts automatically
- block manual discount edits after the bill is printed

When the system applies the policy, staff don't have to remember it, and customers see the same price every time.

## Track it weekly

Look at:

- total discount as a percentage of sales
- discount by staff member
- bills with above-limit discounts and their reasons
- margin after discount by category

A sudden rise for one user or one day is worth a conversation. A steady rise overall means the policy needs revisiting.

## Competing without discount wars

Matching every online discount is a race a neighbourhood pharmacy rarely wins. Compete on what customers value beyond price:

- medicines available today, not in two days
- advice from a pharmacist who knows them
- home delivery for regulars
- refill reminders
- a loyalty programme that rewards repeat visits — see [pharmacy loyalty programmes](/resources/blog/pharmacy-loyalty-programme/)

Most regular patients will pay a fair price for reliability and service.

## Rules to keep in mind

Never sell above MRP, and apply discounts consistently with any applicable regulations on medicine pricing and promotion.

## The outcome

A clear discount policy, enforced by software and reviewed weekly, turns discounting from a habit into a business decision. Margins become predictable, staff stop negotiating at the counter, and customers trust that everyone pays the same fair price.
