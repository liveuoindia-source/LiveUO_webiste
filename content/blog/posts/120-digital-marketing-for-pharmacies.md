A neighbourhood pharmacy has advantages no online pharmacy can match: medicines today, a pharmacist who knows the patient, and delivery within the hour. Digital marketing is how those advantages get noticed by people nearby — but pharmacies have to market more carefully than most businesses.

## Know the boundaries

Medicines are regulated, and so is how they're promoted. In India:

- promotion of prescription medicines to the public is restricted
- certain advertisements and claims about curing specific diseases are prohibited by law
- discounts and offers must respect MRP and applicable rules

So pharmacy marketing works best when it focuses on **the pharmacy** — services, availability, convenience, expertise — and on **general health information**, rather than on promoting specific prescription products. When in doubt, check with your state drug control authority or a professional association.

## 1. Google Business Profile

For a pharmacy, this is the most important channel. People search "pharmacy near me" or "medical store open now" when they need something urgently.

- correct name, address and phone
- accurate hours, including late nights and holidays
- services: home delivery, health checks, vaccines if offered, wheelchair access
- photos of the storefront and interior
- steady reviews, with replies

See [Google Business Profile checklist](/resources/blog/google-business-profile-optimisation/).

## 2. WhatsApp

- a click-to-chat link for orders: "Send your prescription on WhatsApp"
- delivery updates and order-ready messages
- refill reminders for chronic patients, with consent — see [refill reminders](/resources/blog/pharmacy-refill-reminders-customer-retention/)

Keep marketing broadcasts rare and useful. See [WhatsApp marketing that customers don't block](/resources/blog/whatsapp-marketing-opt-in/).

## 3. Local content

Useful, accurate information builds trust:

- seasonal advice — monsoon hygiene, heat safety, flu season
- how to store insulin, how to use an inhaler correctly
- what to keep in a home first-aid kit
- availability of health check-ups or camps

Share on Google posts, Instagram and Facebook. Avoid recommending prescription medicines or making treatment claims.

## 4. Reviews

Ask satisfied customers for reviews, reply to all of them, and fix what complaints reveal — slow service, stock-outs, rude behaviour. See [online reviews](/resources/blog/online-reviews-reputation-management/).

## 5. Loyalty and retention

Your regular patients are your biggest asset. A simple loyalty programme, refill reminders and home delivery keep them coming back. See [pharmacy loyalty programmes](/resources/blog/pharmacy-loyalty-programme/).

## 6. Local ads, carefully

Small, local campaigns promoting your **services** — home delivery, 24-hour opening, health check-ups — can work well. Ad platforms have their own policies for pharmacy and healthcare advertising, and some require certification. Check before running.

## Protect patient privacy

Customer phone numbers and purchase histories can reveal health conditions. Collect consent for messages, limit access, and never use health data for unrelated marketing.

## Measure

- calls and direction requests from Google
- WhatsApp orders per week
- new reviews and rating
- repeat customers

A pharmacy doesn't need aggressive advertising. It needs to be easy to find, easy to order from, and trusted. Digital marketing, done within the rules, delivers exactly that.
