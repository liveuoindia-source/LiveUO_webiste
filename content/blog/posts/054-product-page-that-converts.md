A product page has one job: give a shopper enough confidence to buy without touching the product. Every element should either answer a question they'd ask in a shop, or remove a reason to hesitate.

Most underperforming product pages aren't badly designed. They just leave questions unanswered.

## Above the fold

On a phone, the first screen should show:

- **a clear main photo** of the product
- **the product name**, specific enough to search — "Cotton kurta, straight fit, navy", not "Classic Kurta"
- **the price**, including any discount shown honestly
- **variant choices** — size, colour, pack size — with availability
- **the delivery estimate** for the shopper's PIN code
- **one primary button**: Add to cart or Buy now

If shoppers have to scroll to find the price or the button, many won't.

## Photos that replace touch

Photos do most of the selling. Include:

- front, back and side views
- close-ups of material, texture, labels or ingredients
- the product in use, or worn
- something that shows scale
- every variant, so colours aren't guessed

Use consistent lighting and backgrounds, and compress images so pages load quickly on mobile networks.

## Key facts before the story

Shoppers skim. Lead with a short list of the facts that decide a purchase:

- size and dimensions, with a size chart
- material, ingredients or specifications
- what's in the box
- care, usage or compatibility

Then add the longer description for people who want detail — what it's for, who it suits, why it's made the way it is. Write it yourself; manufacturer copy pasted across many sites gives search engines nothing new.

## Price clarity

- Show the final price including GST, as Indian consumers expect.
- If there's a discount, show the original price and the saving.
- Mention delivery charges or the free-delivery threshold.
- Show COD availability and any COD fee before checkout.

Surprises at checkout lose sales that the product page already won.

## Delivery and returns

Next to the buy button, answer:

- when will it arrive? (estimated date, by PIN code)
- can I return or exchange it, and how?
- is COD available?

Link to the full policy, but put the one-line summary where the decision is being made.

## Social proof

Reviews with photos from real customers are among the strongest signals on a page. Show:

- average rating and number of reviews
- recent reviews first, with a way to filter
- customer photos where available
- your replies to critical reviews

Never fabricate reviews. Shoppers have become good at spotting them, and it damages trust in everything else on the page.

## Answer objections before they're asked

Look at the questions customers send by WhatsApp or email, and answer them on the page:

- "Will this fit…?"
- "Is it suitable for…?"
- "How long does it last?"

A short FAQ section per product often lifts conversion and reduces support messages at the same time.

## Speed and stability

A slow page loses buyers before they see anything. Test on a mid-range phone over a mobile network. Lazy-load images below the fold, avoid heavy scripts, and make sure the layout doesn't jump as it loads — shoppers tap the wrong thing and leave.

## Measure, then improve

Track for each product:

- views
- add-to-cart rate
- purchase rate
- return rate and reasons

A product with many views and few add-to-carts has a page problem. One with many add-to-carts and few purchases has a checkout or price problem. One with high returns has a description problem — the page promised something the product didn't deliver.

Good product pages are built from customer questions. Every answer you add removes one more reason not to buy.
