B2B buyers rarely buy on impulse. A purchase manager evaluating suppliers, an IT head comparing software, or a factory owner looking for a service partner will usually research for weeks — searching, reading, comparing, asking colleagues — before talking to anyone.

Content marketing puts your business in front of them during that research, and gives them reasons to trust you before the first call.

## Start with the questions buyers ask

The best topics come from your sales and support teams. Ask:

- What do prospects ask in the first meeting?
- What do they misunderstand about the product or service?
- What objections come up most?
- What do they compare you with?
- What problems lead them to look for a supplier?

Each answer is a potential article. Questions buyers ask your team are questions they also type into Google.

## Match content to the buying stage

| Stage | Buyer's question | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | "Why are we losing money on X?" | Explainers, guides |
| Options | "What are the ways to fix it?" | Comparisons, frameworks |
| Decision | "Why this supplier?" | Case notes, pricing guidance, FAQs |

Many companies only write decision-stage content — brochures in article form. The early stages are where most buyers start.

## Write for decision-makers

- **Be specific.** Numbers, steps, examples, real constraints. Generic advice is everywhere.
- **Be honest about trade-offs.** Saying when your approach isn't right builds credibility.
- **Get to the point.** Busy readers skim. Use clear headings, short paragraphs and tables.
- **Use your expertise.** Interview your engineers, consultants or operations team. Their knowledge is what competitors can't copy.

## Make it findable

- one main topic per article, reflected in the title and headings
- answer the core question early
- link related articles to each other and to the relevant service page
- keep pages fast and mobile-friendly
- update important articles when things change

## Turn readers into leads

Not everyone is ready to talk. Offer next steps for different readiness levels:

- a related article to keep reading
- a checklist, template or calculator in exchange for an email
- a newsletter for ongoing updates
- a consultation or demo for those ready now

## Distribute it

Search takes time. Meanwhile:

- share articles on LinkedIn from the company and from team members
- give sales a library of articles to send prospects
- include new content in a monthly newsletter
- repurpose key points into short posts or videos

## Measure what matters

- organic traffic to articles over time
- searches you rank for
- leads that read content before enquiring
- articles sales actually use
- conversions from content offers

Traffic is a means; qualified enquiries are the end.

## A sustainable plan

Start with ten articles answering your most frequent buyer questions. Publish one a week. After three months, check which bring traffic and enquiries, and write more on those themes.

B2B content marketing compounds slowly and then keeps working. The companies that win are the ones that keep publishing genuinely useful material long after others have given up.
