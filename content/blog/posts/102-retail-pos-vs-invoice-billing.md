"Billing software" covers two very different jobs. A busy shop counter needs to bill a customer in seconds. A distributor or service company needs detailed invoices with customer terms, delivery details and payment tracking. Software built for one often struggles with the other.

## POS billing: built for speed

Typical users: retail shops, pharmacies, restaurants, supermarkets.

Needs:

- barcode scanning and fast item search
- keyboard shortcuts
- quick customer lookup by phone number
- multiple payment modes on one bill — cash, UPI, card
- receipt printer support
- cash drawer and day-end closing
- offline operation when the internet drops

Success is measured in seconds per bill and queue length.

## Invoice billing: built for detail

Typical users: wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers, service businesses.

Needs:

- customer master with GSTIN, billing and shipping addresses
- customer-specific price lists and credit terms
- quotations and sales orders
- partial deliveries and delivery challans
- e-invoicing and e-way bills where applicable
- outstanding tracking and reminders
- A4 invoice formats with terms and bank details

Success is measured in accuracy, compliance and getting paid on time.

## Side by side

| | POS | Invoice |
|---|---|---|
| Speed per bill | Critical | Less critical |
| Customer details | Optional | Essential |
| Payment | Immediate | Often on credit |
| Document | Receipt-style invoice | Detailed A4 invoice |
| Delivery | At counter | Shipped, often in parts |
| Key reports | Day-end, cash by mode | Outstanding, ageing |

## Many businesses need both

A hardware store sells to walk-in customers at the counter and to contractors on credit. A pharmacy serves patients at the counter and supplies clinics on monthly bills. A bakery runs a counter and takes bulk orders from offices.

For these businesses, the right software offers:

- a fast POS screen for walk-ins
- a full invoice screen for business customers
- one shared stock
- one set of accounts and GST reports

Running two separate systems means duplicated items, stock that never matches, and double work at month-end.

## Questions to ask vendors

- How many seconds does a typical counter bill take?
- Does billing work offline?
- Can business customers have their own price lists and credit limits?
- Are quotations, orders and partial deliveries supported?
- Is e-invoicing built in?
- Do POS and invoice sales share stock and reports?

## Choosing

Start with where most of your billing time goes. If it's the counter, prioritise speed and test it under real conditions. If it's business customers, prioritise detail, credit control and compliance. If it's both, insist on software that handles both without forcing one into the other's screen.
