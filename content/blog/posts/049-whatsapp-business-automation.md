For many Indian customers, WhatsApp is the channel they actually read. An email about an order update may go unopened; a WhatsApp message usually gets seen within minutes. That makes it powerful for businesses — and easy to misuse.

Automation helps when it removes repetitive messages your team sends all day. It hurts when it traps customers in a bot or floods them with promotions.

## Messages worth automating

### Transactional updates

These are the safest and most appreciated:

- order confirmations and dispatch updates with tracking links
- appointment confirmations and reminders
- payment receipts and due-date reminders
- delivery attempts and rescheduling

They answer a question the customer would otherwise call about.

### Common questions

Opening hours, location, price lists, documents needed, return policy. A simple menu or AI assistant can answer these instantly, at any hour.

### Lead capture

When someone messages from an ad or website, an automated flow can ask a few qualifying questions — product, location, budget, preferred time — and route the lead to the right salesperson with all details attached.

### Follow-ups your team forgets

Quotes not responded to, demos not booked, renewals coming up. Scheduled, polite follow-ups recover business that otherwise slips away.

## Keep these with a person

- complaints and refunds
- anything involving health, legal or financial advice
- negotiations and custom quotes
- customers who are clearly frustrated

A good flow always offers a way to reach a person, and hands the conversation over with its history, so the customer doesn't repeat themselves.

## The rules you must follow

WhatsApp has strict policies for businesses, and your number can be restricted if customers block or report it.

- **Opt-in first.** Collect clear consent to message the customer on WhatsApp — at checkout, on a form, or when they message you.
- **Templates outside the conversation window.** When you start a conversation, or message after the customer's last message has gone quiet for a while, you must use a pre-approved template.
- **Categories matter.** Templates are categorised — for example utility, authentication or marketing — with different rules and pricing.
- **Easy opt-out.** Let customers stop messages easily, and honour it immediately.
- **Privacy.** Collect only what you need, and handle personal data in line with India's data protection law.

## Choosing the setup

| Need | Option |
|---|---|
| One shop, a few chats a day | WhatsApp Business app, with quick replies and labels |
| Several staff answering | WhatsApp Business Platform with a shared inbox |
| Automated updates from your systems | Platform connected to your billing, CRM or store |
| Lead qualification and FAQs | Platform with a chatbot flow |

The Platform is accessed directly or through a provider. Choose one that connects to the systems you already use, so messages are triggered by real events — an invoice saved, an order shipped — rather than typed by hand.

## Design flows people don't hate

- **Short messages.** One idea per message.
- **Buttons over typing.** Quick-reply buttons are faster than free text.
- **Clear exits.** "Talk to a person" should always be an option.
- **No dead ends.** If the bot doesn't understand, it should hand over, not loop.
- **Sensible timing.** No promotional messages late at night.

## Measure it

Track response time, how many queries are resolved without a person, how many leads are qualified, opt-out and block rates, and the cost per conversation. A rising block rate is an early warning that messages feel like spam.

Start with one high-volume, low-risk flow — order updates or appointment reminders — get it right, then expand. WhatsApp automation works best when customers barely notice it: they just get the right message at the right time.
