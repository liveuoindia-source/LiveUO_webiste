For many small businesses, the problem isn't sales — it's collecting the money from sales. Invoices go out, customers delay, and the business ends up financing its customers while struggling to pay its own suppliers.

Getting paid on time is mostly about routine: clear terms, consistent reminders and follow-through.

## Start with clear terms

Put payment terms on every quotation, order and invoice:

- due date, not just "30 days"
- accepted payment methods, with bank and UPI details
- what happens if payment is late
- who to contact about billing questions

Agree credit limits and credit days for regular customers in writing.

## Make paying easy

- include UPI ID or QR code on invoices
- add a payment link in reminder messages
- send invoices promptly — late invoices get paid late
- make sure invoices are correct; disputes delay payment

## Automate reminders

A simple schedule:

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| 3 days before due | Friendly reminder with invoice and payment link |
| On due date | "Payment due today" |
| 7 days overdue | Polite follow-up |
| 15 days overdue | Firm reminder, mention credit terms |
| 30 days overdue | Personal call from the owner or manager |

Send by WhatsApp and email. Automation ensures every customer gets reminded consistently — not just the ones someone remembers.

## Use the ageing report weekly

An ageing report groups outstanding amounts by age:

| Customer | 0–30 | 31–60 | 61–90 | 90+ | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Traders | 45,000 | — | — | — | 45,000 |
| B Stores | 12,000 | 18,000 | — | — | 30,000 |
| C Enterprises | — | — | 22,000 | 15,000 | 37,000 |

Focus on the oldest buckets first. The longer an invoice stays unpaid, the less likely it is to be paid in full.

## Credit control

- set credit limits and credit days per customer
- warn or block new invoices that exceed limits
- allow overrides only with approval and a reason
- review limits for slow payers

## Match receipts properly

Record payments against specific invoices, not just as a lump sum on the account. Otherwise disputes arise about which invoices are paid, and ageing reports become unreliable.

## Escalation

For persistent non-payers:

1. personal call from a senior person
2. hold further supplies until the account is regularised
3. agree a written payment plan if needed
4. formal notice, and legal advice as a last resort

Most customers pay before step 2 when follow-up is consistent.

## Measure

- total overdue amount, trending down
- average days to collect
- share of invoices paid on time
- customers repeatedly overdue

## The habit

A weekly thirty-minute collections review, supported by automatic reminders, transforms cash flow for most small businesses. The customers don't change — the consistency does.
