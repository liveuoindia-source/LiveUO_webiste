"Will it pay for itself?" is the right question to ask about any automation project. The honest answer needs numbers from your own operation — not a vendor's percentage — and an equally honest view of costs.

## Measure the current process

Before estimating savings, measure today:

- **volume** — how many times per month
- **time** — minutes per instance, including corrections
- **people** — who does it, at what cost
- **errors** — how often, and what each one costs to fix
- **delays** — how long the process takes end to end, and what delay costs

Two weeks of measurement beats any assumption.

## Benefits to count

### Time saved

Hours freed per month × cost per hour. Be clear how the time will be used: more volume handled without hiring, faster service, or work previously left undone.

### Errors avoided

Errors per month × average cost per error. Include rework, refunds, penalties and lost customers where relevant.

### Faster cycles

Shorter turnaround can be worth money: faster invoicing means faster payment; faster lead response means more sales.

### Revenue protected or gained

Follow-ups that no longer slip, renewals that aren't missed, orders processed out of hours.

### Risk reduced

Compliance and audit benefits are harder to quantify but real. Note them even if you don't put a number on them.

## Costs to count

| Cost | Examples |
|---|---|
| One-time | Process redesign, data clean-up, build or setup, integration, testing, training |
| Recurring | Licences, hosting, usage fees, support |
| Internal | Staff time during setup and early weeks |

## A worked example

Invoice data entry:

- 1,200 invoices a month
- 4 minutes each manually → 80 hours a month
- after automation: 1 minute review each → 20 hours a month
- **time saved: 60 hours a month**

Errors:

- 2% of invoices had errors costing on average ₹500 to resolve → 24 × ₹500 = ₹12,000 a month
- after automation, assume errors fall by half → **₹6,000 a month saved**

If staff cost is ₹300 an hour, time saved is worth ₹18,000 a month. Total monthly benefit: about ₹24,000.

If one-time cost is ₹2,50,000 and recurring cost ₹6,000 a month, net monthly benefit is ₹18,000, and **payback is about 14 months**.

Your numbers will differ. The method is what matters.

## Avoid common traps

- **Assuming 100% automation.** Most processes keep a person for exceptions.
- **Ignoring adoption time.** Benefits ramp up over weeks, not on day one.
- **Counting hours that won't be used.** Freed time only has value if it's redeployed.
- **Forgetting recurring costs.**
- **Using vendor benchmarks instead of your own data.**

## Measure after launch

Compare actual volume, time and errors with the baseline at one, three and six months. Use the results to improve the automation and to decide on the next project. See [which processes to automate first](/resources/blog/which-processes-to-automate-first/).

A business case built on your own measurements is more convincing to decision-makers — and more likely to be right.
