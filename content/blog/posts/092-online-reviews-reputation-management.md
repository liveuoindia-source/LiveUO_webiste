Before calling a clinic, visiting a shop or hiring a service, most people check reviews. A business with steady, recent, positive reviews — and thoughtful replies to the occasional negative one — earns trust before saying a word.

## Get more reviews, honestly

### Ask at the right moment

Ask when the customer is happiest: after a successful service, on delivery, or after they've thanked you.

### Make it easy

- a short link to your review page
- a QR code at the counter or on the bill
- a message after purchase with the link

The fewer steps, the more reviews.

### Ask everyone

Ask every customer, not just the ones you expect to be positive. Selectively asking only happy customers is against some platforms' guidelines and creates an unrealistic picture.

### Never fake or buy

Fake reviews, paid reviews and rewards for positive reviews break platform rules and can lead to penalties. Customers increasingly spot them, too.

## Respond to every review

### Positive reviews

Thank the person by name if possible, and mention something specific. Keep it short and genuine.

### Negative reviews

1. **Respond promptly** — within a day or two.
2. **Stay calm.** Don't argue or blame.
3. **Acknowledge** the experience.
4. **Explain briefly** if there's context, without being defensive.
5. **Offer to resolve it** offline — give a name and contact.
6. **Follow through.**

Example:

> Thank you for your feedback, and sorry your order arrived late. That's not the experience we want for you. Please call our manager Ravi on [number] and we'll make it right.

A calm, helpful reply often reassures future customers more than another five-star review.

### Fake or abusive reviews

If a review breaks platform policy — spam, abusive language, from a competitor — report it through the platform. Reply politely in the meantime if needed.

## Learn from reviews

Reviews are free feedback. Every month:

- read recent reviews
- note recurring praise — what customers value
- note recurring complaints — what needs fixing
- share both with the team

Fixing the cause of a common complaint improves future reviews more than any reply.

## Where reviews matter

- Google Business Profile — most important for local businesses
- marketplaces, for online sellers
- industry platforms and directories
- social media pages

Focus on the one or two places your customers actually look.

## Track progress

- average rating
- number of new reviews per month
- share of reviews replied to
- common themes

A steady routine — ask every customer, reply to every review, fix what reviews reveal — builds a reputation that keeps working for the business every day.
