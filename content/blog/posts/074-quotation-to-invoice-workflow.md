In many small businesses, quotations live in Word or Excel, orders in WhatsApp chats, and invoices in the billing software. Each step is retyped. Prices drift between the quote and the bill, follow-ups depend on memory, and nobody can say how many quotes are pending this month.

Connecting the steps fixes most of that.

## The flow

1. **Enquiry** — what the customer wants.
2. **Quotation** — items, quantities, rates, taxes, validity and terms.
3. **Follow-up** — until the customer decides.
4. **Sales order** — once accepted.
5. **Delivery** — full or partial, with a delivery challan where needed.
6. **Invoice** — the GST tax invoice, for what was delivered.
7. **Payment** — recorded against the invoice.

Each step should create the next from the same data, without retyping.

## Quotations that work

A good quotation includes:

- your business details and GSTIN
- customer details
- item descriptions, quantities and rates
- applicable taxes, shown clearly
- validity date
- delivery time and payment terms
- a reference number for follow-up

Create quotes from the item master so rates and taxes are consistent with what you'll invoice.

## Track every quote

A quotation register shows:

| Quote | Customer | Value | Sent | Valid until | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q-0412 | Shree Traders | ₹1,85,000 | 03 Nov | 18 Nov | Follow-up due |
| Q-0415 | Metro Clinic | ₹42,500 | 05 Nov | 20 Nov | Accepted |
| Q-0418 | City Hardware | ₹96,000 | 07 Nov | 22 Nov | Lost — price |

Schedule follow-up reminders automatically. Record why quotes are lost — price, timing, competitor, scope — to learn from them.

## Convert, don't retype

When a quote is accepted, convert it into a sales order with one click. Changes agreed with the customer are made on the order, with the original quote kept for reference.

## Partial deliveries

Orders are often delivered in parts. The system should:

- invoice each delivery against the order
- show delivered, invoiced and pending quantities
- prevent invoicing more than ordered without approval

## Proforma invoices

Some customers need a proforma invoice before paying in advance. It's not a tax invoice — make that clear on the document — and the tax invoice is issued when supply happens. Advance receipts may have their own GST implications depending on what you supply, so check with your tax adviser.

## Measure the pipeline

With quotes in the system, you can see:

- total value of open quotes
- conversion rate by product, customer type or salesperson
- average time from quote to decision
- reasons for lost quotes

These numbers help forecast sales and show where pricing or follow-up needs work.

## The payoff

A connected quote-to-invoice workflow means prices match from first offer to final bill, nothing is retyped, follow-ups happen on time, and you know your pipeline at a glance. For a growing business, that's the difference between hoping for sales and managing them.
