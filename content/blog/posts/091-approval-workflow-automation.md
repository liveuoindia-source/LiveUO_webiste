In many businesses, approvals happen on WhatsApp: a photo of a quotation, "OK" from the owner, and the purchase goes ahead. It's fast — until someone asks who approved what, at what price, and the answer is buried in a chat from three months ago.

Approval workflows keep the speed and add structure.

## Common approvals

- purchase requests and orders above a value
- discounts beyond the counter limit
- expense claims and reimbursements
- credit limit overrides
- price and master data changes
- leave and attendance corrections
- vendor payments

## How a workflow works

1. **Request** — submitted in a form with the required details and attachments.
2. **Rules** — decide who must approve, based on type, value and department.
3. **Notification** — approver is alerted on phone or email.
4. **Decision** — approve, reject or ask for more information, with comments.
5. **Escalation** — if no response within a set time.
6. **Action** — once approved, the next step happens automatically: a purchase order is created, a discount applied, a payment scheduled.
7. **Record** — every step is logged.

## Write the rules

| Request | Condition | Approver |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase | Up to a set amount | Department head |
| Purchase | Above it | Owner or finance |
| Discount | Above counter limit | Store manager |
| Expense | Any | Manager, then accounts |
| Credit override | Any | Finance head |

Keep rules simple. Too many levels slow everything down.

## Auto-approve the routine

Set thresholds below which requests are approved automatically — low-value, standard items from approved suppliers. Approvers then spend time only on what needs judgment.

## Make approving easy

- one-tap approve or reject on a phone
- all information on one screen: amount, reason, attachments, budget remaining
- reminders and escalation, so nothing waits indefinitely
- delegation when someone is on leave

## Benefits

- **speed** — no waiting to catch the owner in person
- **control** — spending follows agreed limits
- **visibility** — pending requests and decisions in one place
- **audit** — who approved what, when and why
- **fewer disputes** — decisions are documented

## Getting started

1. Choose one approval type that happens often and causes friction — purchases or expenses.
2. Write the rules.
3. Set up the form and workflow.
4. Run it for a month and adjust thresholds.
5. Add the next approval type.

Approval workflows don't add bureaucracy when designed well. They remove the chasing, the guesswork and the "I thought you'd approved it" — and give owners control without being a bottleneck.
