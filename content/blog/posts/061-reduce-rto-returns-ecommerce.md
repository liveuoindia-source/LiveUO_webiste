For many Indian online stores, the orders that come back cost more than the ones that never happen. Shipping both ways, packaging, unsellable stock and days of locked-up inventory turn a healthy-looking order count into a thin or negative margin.

Most returns and RTO fall into a few patterns, and each has practical fixes.

## Why orders come back

**Before delivery (RTO):**

- customer refuses a COD order
- customer unreachable or not at the address
- address incomplete or wrong
- order placed on impulse, or not genuine

**After delivery (returns):**

- wrong size or fit
- product different from what the page suggested
- damaged in transit
- changed mind

Track reasons separately. The fixes for "refused COD" and "wrong size" have nothing in common.

## Fixes before dispatch

### Confirm risky orders

For COD orders — especially high-value ones or from pin codes with high RTO — send an automatic WhatsApp message asking the customer to confirm with one tap. Orders not confirmed within a set time can be cancelled or called. Many impulse orders end here, before shipping costs are spent.

### Validate addresses

- auto-fill city and state from the PIN code
- require a house number and a landmark
- flag addresses that look incomplete for a quick check
- confirm phone numbers by OTP where it makes sense

### Use your own history

Restrict COD for:

- pin codes with consistently high RTO
- customers who have refused orders before
- orders above a value threshold

Offer prepaid with a small incentive instead.

## Fixes on the product page

Most post-delivery returns trace back to a page that didn't set the right expectation.

- **Accurate photos** in natural light, showing true colour and texture.
- **Size charts with measurements**, not just S/M/L, plus fit notes such as "runs small".
- **Specific descriptions** — materials, dimensions, what's included.
- **Reviews that mention fit or quality**, surfaced near the size selector.

See [anatomy of a product page that converts](/resources/blog/product-page-that-converts/) for more.

## Fixes in packing and delivery

- pack fragile items properly, and photograph high-value packs before dispatch
- set clear delivery expectations and send updates on the day
- ask couriers for re-attempts before marking a parcel undeliverable
- choose courier partners by delivered rate in each region, not just price

## A smarter returns policy

Generous returns build trust, but the details matter:

- **offer exchange first** for size issues
- **require unused condition and original tags**
- **collect a reason** for every return, with photos for damage claims
- **refund to the original payment method**, or offer store credit with a small bonus

## Measure by segment

Track RTO and return rates by:

- payment method
- pin code and region
- product and category
- courier partner
- traffic source — some campaigns bring browsers, not buyers

You'll often find a few products, regions or campaigns account for a large share of returns.

## Build it into the system

Order confirmation, COD rules, address checks and return reasons are far easier to run when they're part of your store and order system rather than manual steps. A store built around your own data can apply them automatically to every order.

Returns will never be zero. But a store that confirms, validates, describes accurately and learns from its own data can bring them down sharply — and every avoided return is margin you keep.
