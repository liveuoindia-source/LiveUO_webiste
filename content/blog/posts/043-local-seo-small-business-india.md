When someone searches for a service with an area name — "dentist Basavanagudi", "AC repair JP Nagar", "chartered accountant near me" — Google tries to show businesses that are relevant, close and well regarded. Local SEO is the work of making sure your business is one of them.

For most small businesses, it's the most valuable marketing channel available, because it reaches people at the moment they're ready to buy.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

The map results usually appear above the regular results for local searches. A complete, accurate profile with real photos and steady reviews is the foundation. We've covered it step by step in [our Google Business Profile checklist](/resources/blog/google-business-profile-optimisation/).

Everything below builds on that.

## Keep your business details consistent

Your name, address and phone number — often called NAP — should be identical everywhere they appear:

- your website, ideally in the footer of every page
- your Google Business Profile
- Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn pages
- directories such as Justdial, IndiaMART or industry listings

Small differences — "3rd Main" in one place and "Third Main Road" in another, or an old phone number on a directory — make it harder for search engines to trust which details are correct. Pick one format and use it everywhere.

## Make your website clearly local

Search engines need to understand where you are and what you do. On your website:

- **Mention your location naturally** in page titles, headings and text — "GST billing software support in Bengaluru", not keyword lists.
- **Add a contact page** with your full address, phone, hours and an embedded map.
- **Add LocalBusiness structured data** so search engines read your details accurately.
- **Make it fast and mobile-friendly.** Most local searches happen on phones.
- **Make calling easy** with a tap-to-call number and WhatsApp link.

## Create pages for each service

A single "Services" page listing everything rarely ranks for anything specific. Give each main service its own page that explains what it is, who it's for, pricing guidance if possible, and common questions. A page about "laptop screen replacement" can rank for that search; a list item can't.

## Location pages — only when genuine

If you have several branches, give each its own page with its address, hours, phone, photos and directions. If you serve areas without a branch, create area pages only where you can say something real: projects done there, local response times, specific services.

Pages that are identical apart from the area name tend to perform poorly and can look like spam.

## Reviews and reputation

Reviews influence both rankings and decisions. Build a habit of asking every satisfied customer, make it easy with a link or QR code, and reply to every review. Steady, recent reviews matter more than a large number collected long ago.

## Earn local mentions and links

Links from local sources help search engines see you as a genuine part of the area:

- local business associations and chambers of commerce
- suppliers or partners who list their dealers or clients
- local news coverage, events and sponsorships
- community groups and resident welfare associations

A few relevant local links are worth more than many irrelevant ones. Avoid buying links.

## Write content local customers search for

Answer the questions customers ask you every week:

- "How much does it cost to … in Bengaluru?"
- "How long does … take?"
- "What documents do I need for …?"

Each well-answered question can bring searches for months, and it shows customers you know your work.

## Measure what matters

Track:

- calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Google Business Profile
- organic traffic to your service and location pages
- enquiries by source, so you know which pages actually bring customers
- your rankings for a handful of important local searches, checked monthly

## A 90-day starting plan

| Month | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Complete the profile, fix NAP everywhere, contact page and structured data |
| 2 | Service pages for your top three services, review requests to every customer |
| 3 | Answer five common customer questions as articles, pursue local links |

Local SEO rarely produces overnight results. It compounds: each review, page and link adds to the last. Businesses that keep at it steadily for six months are usually the ones that own their area's searches.
