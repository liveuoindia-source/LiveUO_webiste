When a business decides to "do something about Google", the first question is usually whether to pay for ads or invest in SEO. Both put you in front of people searching for what you sell. They work very differently.

## Google Ads

You pay for each click on ads shown at the top and bottom of search results.

**Strengths:**

- traffic starts as soon as campaigns are live
- precise control over keywords, locations and schedule
- easy to test offers and landing pages
- clear measurement of cost per lead

**Limitations:**

- traffic stops when you stop paying
- costs rise in competitive categories
- requires ongoing management

## SEO

You improve your website so it ranks in the organic, unpaid results.

**Strengths:**

- no cost per click
- traffic builds and compounds over time
- organic results are trusted by many searchers
- useful content also supports sales and reputation

**Limitations:**

- takes months to build
- no guaranteed positions
- requires consistent effort on content, technical health and reputation

## Comparison

| | Google Ads | SEO |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Immediate | Months |
| Cost model | Per click | Effort and content |
| When you stop | Traffic stops | Traffic declines slowly |
| Control | High | Indirect |
| Best for | Immediate demand, testing | Long-term, sustainable traffic |

## Which suits your situation?

**Lean towards ads first if:**

- you need enquiries now
- you're launching a new service or location
- your margins can support paid clicks
- you want to test which searches bring customers

**Lean towards SEO first if:**

- you can wait for results
- clicks in your category are very expensive
- you have expertise to share in content
- you want a lasting asset rather than ongoing spend

## The combined approach

For most businesses, the best answer is both, in sequence:

1. **Run focused ads** to bring enquiries and learn which keywords convert. See [Google Ads on a small budget](/resources/blog/google-ads-small-budget/).
2. **Fix SEO foundations** — site speed, service pages, Google Business Profile, local SEO.
3. **Create content** around the searches ads proved valuable.
4. **Reduce ad spend** on searches where organic rankings become strong, and redirect budget to new opportunities.

Ads data makes SEO smarter; SEO reduces dependence on ads over time.

## Local businesses

For local services, the Google Business Profile and local SEO often bring more enquiries than either ads or organic website rankings. Start there — see [local SEO for small businesses](/resources/blog/local-seo-small-business-india/).

## Measure both the same way

Track enquiries and customers from each channel, not just traffic. Compare cost per customer: ad spend for ads, and content and effort for SEO.

The question isn't really SEO *or* ads. It's how much to put into each, and when. Start with what your cash flow and timeline need, and build towards a mix where paid and organic support each other.
