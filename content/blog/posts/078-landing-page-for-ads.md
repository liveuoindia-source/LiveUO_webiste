Paying for a click and sending it to a page that doesn't convert is one of the most common ways ad budgets are wasted. The ad did its job; the page didn't. A focused landing page is often the cheapest improvement you can make to any campaign.

## Match the message

The headline should continue the conversation the ad started. If the ad says "Same-day AC repair in Jayanagar", the page should open with exactly that — not a generic welcome. When the page doesn't match, visitors assume they're in the wrong place and leave.

Create a separate page, or at least a separate headline, for each major ad group.

## One page, one action

Decide what you want visitors to do — call, WhatsApp, fill a form, book a slot — and make that the only main action. Repeat it in a few places as they scroll. Remove the main navigation or keep it minimal, so there are fewer ways to wander off.

## Structure that works

1. **Headline** matching the ad, with the main benefit.
2. **Subheading** with specifics — location, timing, price guidance.
3. **Primary action** — button, form or click-to-call.
4. **Key benefits** in three to five short points.
5. **Proof** — reviews, years in business, clients served, certifications you actually hold.
6. **How it works** — three simple steps.
7. **FAQs** answering the objections that stop people acting.
8. **Repeat the action** at the end.

## Trust signals

For a business people don't know yet:

- real photos of your team, premises or work
- genuine reviews with names (with permission)
- address and phone number
- clear pricing or a price range where possible
- guarantees or warranties you actually offer

Never invent testimonials or statistics. They're easy to spot, and they undermine everything else.

## Mobile first

Most ad clicks come from phones:

- fast loading on a mobile connection
- large, easy-to-tap buttons
- click-to-call and WhatsApp buttons
- short forms with the right keyboards
- no pop-ups that cover the content

Test it on an ordinary phone, not just a desktop.

## Forms

Ask only what you need. Name and phone, plus one qualifying question — service, location or budget — is often enough. Confirm submission clearly, and send an instant acknowledgement by WhatsApp or email.

## Tracking

Track the actions that matter:

- form submissions
- clicks on call and WhatsApp buttons
- calls from the page, if you use call tracking

Send these as conversions to Google Ads or Meta so campaigns optimise for enquiries, not clicks. Test tracking yourself before launch.

## Test and improve

Once traffic is steady, test one change at a time: headline, offer, form length, button text, proof. Measure conversion rate and lead quality — a change that brings more leads of poorer quality isn't a win.

## Common mistakes

- generic headline unrelated to the ad
- several competing offers on one page
- slow loading from heavy images or scripts
- long forms asking for information you don't use
- no phone number for people who prefer to call
- broken or missing conversion tracking

A good landing page makes every rupee of ad spend work harder. It's often the difference between a campaign that "doesn't work" and one that becomes your most reliable source of customers.
