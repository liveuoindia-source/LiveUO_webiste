A pharmacy franchise model lets a brand grow faster than it could by opening its own stores. It also creates a particular challenge: each store is a separate business, but customers expect the same brand, range and service everywhere.

Software is where that consistency is either enforced or quietly lost.

## What head office needs to control

### One item master

Every franchise store should use the same item master — names, compositions, HSN codes, pack sizes. When stores create their own items, the same medicine ends up under five names, reports become meaningless, and supply planning breaks.

Head office should own the master; stores should request new items rather than create them.

### Pricing and discounts

Franchisors usually want consistent selling prices and discount policies across the brand. Software can:

- push a standard price list to all stores
- limit discount percentages at the counter
- require approval for discounts above a threshold
- prevent sales above MRP

### Supply

Most pharmacy franchise models include supply from the franchisor's warehouse or approved distributors. Software should let stores raise indents to head office, receive stock with full batch and expiry detail, and track what's outstanding.

### Visibility

Head office needs enough data to support the store and calculate any fees or royalties: sales, stock levels, near-expiry stock, purchases outside the approved supply chain. The franchise agreement should define what is shared.

## What stays with the franchisee

Franchisees own their business. They typically handle:

- their own drug licence and registered pharmacist
- their own GST registration and returns
- their own staff, cash and bank
- day-to-day operations within brand standards

The software should respect that boundary: franchisees see their own store; head office sees what the agreement allows.

## Data and privacy

Customer data — names, phone numbers, prescriptions — is sensitive. Agree in writing:

- who controls customer data collected at franchise stores
- what head office may access, and for what purpose
- how consent is collected for marketing messages
- how data is protected and deleted

India's data protection law makes these questions more than a formality.

## Onboarding a new franchise store

A standard onboarding process prevents most early problems:

1. Create the store with its GSTIN, licence details and address.
2. Assign the standard item master and price list.
3. Set user roles: owner, pharmacist, counter staff.
4. Load opening stock with batch and expiry.
5. Configure supply: which warehouse, which items.
6. Train staff on the standard billing, purchase and return processes.
7. Run supervised for the first weeks, with daily checks from head office.

## Reports a franchisor should watch

- sales and growth by store
- stock value, near-expiry and dead stock by store
- share of purchases through approved supply
- discount levels by store
- stockouts of key items
- compliance indicators, such as missing prescription details for scheduled drugs

## Common problems

- **Stores creating items locally**, breaking the master.
- **Off-system purchases** that bypass approved supply.
- **Discounting wars** between nearby franchise stores.
- **Late or missing data**, if stores use different software or work offline without syncing.

## The core principle

A franchise network is only as consistent as its least disciplined store. One platform, one item master, enforced pricing rules and agreed visibility let a franchisor protect the brand without running every store.
