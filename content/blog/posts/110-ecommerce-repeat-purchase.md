Many online stores spend most of their marketing budget winning first orders and very little on the second. Yet the second order is where profitability usually starts: the acquisition cost has already been paid, and the customer already trusts you.

## The second order starts with the first delivery

Repeat business is decided early:

- the product matches the description
- delivery arrives when promised
- packaging is careful
- any problem is solved quickly

No reminder or loyalty scheme recovers a poor first experience.

## Post-purchase messages

With the customer's consent, send a short sequence:

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| Order placed | Confirmation with expected delivery date |
| Shipped | Tracking link |
| Delivered | How to use or care for the product, support contact |
| A week later | Review request |
| Later | Relevant recommendation or reorder reminder |

Keep each message useful. A care guide or usage tip builds more goodwill than another promotion.

## Reorder reminders for consumables

If you sell products that run out — food, supplements, personal care, pet supplies, refills — estimate how long a typical pack lasts and remind customers just before. Include a one-tap link to reorder the same item.

## Make reordering effortless

- customer accounts with saved addresses and order history
- "buy again" buttons
- saved payment methods where permitted
- login by phone number and OTP

The fewer steps between intent and order, the more repeat orders you get.

## Loyalty and subscriptions

- **points** for each purchase, redeemable later
- **member benefits** like free shipping or early sale access
- **subscriptions** for regular consumables, with easy pause and cancel

Design rewards around your margins, and make them simple to understand.

## Win back lapsed customers

Identify customers who haven't ordered for longer than usual. A personal message — what's new, a reminder of what they bought, sometimes a modest offer — brings some back. Ask those who don't return why; the answers are valuable.

## Measure by cohort

Group customers by the month of their first order and track:

- share who place a second order within 30, 60 and 90 days
- average orders per customer over time
- revenue per customer over time

If newer cohorts repeat more than older ones, your changes are working.

## The economics

| | First order | Repeat order |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition cost | High | Low |
| Trust | Being built | Established |
| Typical profit | Often low or negative | Usually positive |

Stores that grow sustainably are rarely the ones with the cheapest first orders. They're the ones whose customers come back.
