Watch a busy pharmacy counter and you'll see a lot of searching: staff walking along shelves, calling out to colleagues, checking the back room. Every minute spent looking is a minute the queue grows. And when an experienced staff member is on leave, the searching gets worse.

Recording where each medicine is kept — and showing it on screen — is one of the simplest improvements a pharmacy can make.

## Design a location code

Use a short, consistent code:

**Rack – Shelf – Position**, for example `B-3-04`: rack B, shelf 3, position 4.

Label every rack and shelf clearly. Keep codes short enough to read at a glance on the billing screen.

## Choose an organising principle

Common approaches:

- **alphabetical by brand** — easy for new staff
- **by category** — antibiotics, cardiac, diabetic, and so on
- **hybrid** — categories for some sections, alphabetical within each

Whatever you choose, apply these rules:

- **fast movers near the counter**
- **look-alike and sound-alike medicines separated**, with warning labels
- **scheduled drugs stored as required**, with H1 and X items controlled appropriately
- **cold-chain items in the fridge**, mapped to fridge shelves
- **heavy and bulk items low down**

## Record locations in the software

Add the location code to each item in the item master. Do it shelf by shelf: take a shelf, enter its code against every item on it, move to the next. Most pharmacies finish within a few weeks, alongside normal work.

Update the location whenever stock is moved.

## Benefits at the counter

- the location appears when the item is searched
- new staff find items without asking
- picking for home delivery orders is faster
- fewer "we don't have it" mistakes when the item is actually on a different shelf

## Benefits behind the counter

### Stock audits

Count shelf by shelf, with a count sheet sorted by location. Audits become faster and more accurate. See [pharmacy stock audit checklist](/resources/blog/pharmacy-stock-audit-checklist/).

### Expiry checks

Near-expiry reports sorted by location let staff walk the shelves in order and pull items quickly.

### Putting away purchases

Purchase entries can show the location, so new stock goes to the right place first time.

## Keep it accurate

Locations drift when:

- new items are added without a location
- stock is moved during reorganisation
- overflow stock is kept somewhere temporary

Make location a required field for new items, and include a spot-check of a few locations in your weekly routine.

## A small change, a big effect

Rack mapping costs a few weeks of steady effort. In return, the counter moves faster, training is easier, audits are shorter, and the pharmacy depends a little less on the memory of any one person.
