Most pharmacies buy the same items from two or three distributors. Over time, habits form: one distributor for this company, another for that one, a third when something's short. Rates drift, schemes come and go, and nobody compares carefully because the pharmacy is busy.

Your own purchase history already holds the answer to "who gives us the best deal?" — it just needs to be looked at.

## Compare effective cost, not invoice rate

The invoice rate is only part of the picture. Effective cost per unit includes:

- trade discount
- cash or prompt-payment discount
- free quantity — see [accounting for schemes and free quantity](/resources/blog/pharmacy-scheme-free-quantity-accounting/)
- any later credit notes or rebates

Two distributors with the same rate can differ meaningfully once these are included.

## Build a comparison for top items

Take your highest-value items — often a small share of items make up most of purchase value — and compare across distributors over the last few months:

## Look beyond price

### Reliability

How often do orders arrive complete and on time? Short supplies mean lost sales and extra orders elsewhere.

### Expiry dating

Does stock arrive with good remaining shelf life? Short-expiry deliveries become write-offs.

### Credit terms

Longer credit has real value for cash flow.

### Returns

How easily does the distributor accept expiry and breakage returns, and how quickly do credit notes arrive?

### Service

Order cut-off times, delivery frequency, responsiveness on urgent needs.

## Negotiate with data

With concrete numbers, negotiations become specific:

- "On these twenty items, your effective cost is higher than another supplier's."
- "Your fill rate last quarter was lower than others'."
- "We buy this volume from you each month."

Most distributors would rather adjust than lose volume. You often don't need to change suppliers — just show them the comparison.

## Let the software watch for you

- purchase entry highlights rates above the last purchase
- reports show rate history by item and supplier
- reorder suggestions can show the last rate from each supplier

Small rate increases are easy to miss on a long invoice. Software catches them every time.

## Avoid over-switching

Moving every item to whichever distributor is cheapest this week creates complexity and weakens relationships. Many pharmacies do best with a primary distributor per company or category, a secondary as back-up, and periodic reviews.

## The payoff

A quarterly look at your own purchase data, followed by an informed conversation with distributors, often improves margins more than any counter initiative — without changing a single supplier.
