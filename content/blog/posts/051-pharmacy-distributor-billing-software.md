A pharmaceutical distributor runs a very different business from a retail pharmacy, even though both sell medicines. The customer is a business, sales are mostly on credit, prices vary by party, schemes change monthly, and every box that leaves the warehouse must be traceable to a batch.

Retail billing software stretched to fit this usually breaks in the same places: rates, schemes, credit and returns.

## Party-wise rates and discounts

Distributors rarely sell at a single price. Rates and discounts depend on the customer type — retailer, hospital, sub-stockist, institution — and often on the individual party.

The software should support:

- multiple price lists by customer category
- party-specific rates or discounts on selected items
- automatic application of the right rate when the party is selected
- a clear record of who changed a rate and when

A billing clerk choosing rates from memory is a reliable way to lose margin or annoy good customers.

## Schemes and free quantity

Manufacturers' schemes — buy ten, get one free; extra discount above a quantity — are central to pharma distribution. The software must:

- apply schemes automatically when conditions are met
- record free quantity separately from billed quantity
- calculate effective cost and margin correctly, including free goods
- track scheme benefits received from manufacturers versus those passed on to customers

## Credit control

Most distributor sales are on credit, so outstanding balances can grow quietly until one customer becomes a problem.

Essential controls:

- credit limit and credit days per party
- a warning or block when a new bill would exceed them, with override only by authorised users
- ageing reports — 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days
- route-wise and salesman-wise outstanding lists for collections
- receipts matched against specific bills

## Batch-wise dispatch

Every line on a distributor's invoice should carry batch, expiry and MRP. This matters for:

- **recalls**, when you must contact every customer who received a batch
- **expiry returns**, which must be matched to the batch sold
- **FEFO picking** — first expiry, first out — so stock doesn't expire in the warehouse

The software should suggest batches by earliest expiry and prevent dispatch of stock below a minimum remaining shelf life where you've set one.

## Orders, routes and delivery

Distribution is organised around salesmen and delivery routes. Useful features include:

- order booking by salesmen, on a phone app or at the office
- route-wise order consolidation and dispatch planning
- delivery challans and invoices printed per route
- proof of delivery and pending delivery tracking

## Returns and expiry claims

Expiry and breakage returns from retailers are routine. Handle them carefully:

1. accept the return against the original invoice and batch where possible
2. issue a credit note with correct GST treatment
3. move the stock to a non-saleable location
4. consolidate returns by manufacturer and batch
5. claim from the manufacturer within its return policy

Without this, expiry returns become a slow leak between what you credited retailers and what you recovered from manufacturers.

## Compliance and documents

Distributors deal with wholesale drug licences, GST invoices, e-way bills for goods movement above the applicable value, and possibly e-invoicing depending on turnover. The software should generate these documents from the same transaction, not separately.

Schedule H1 and other regulated drugs need sale records by party as required by the rules — make sure your system captures them.

## Reports that run the business

- sales by party, item, manufacturer and salesman
- scheme benefit received vs passed on
- near-expiry stock by manufacturer
- outstanding and ageing by route
- margin by party and by manufacturer

## One system for retail and distribution

Chains that also run a distribution arm benefit from one system across both: stock moves from distribution to their own stores with full batch detail, and owners see the whole business in one place. The key is that the system treats each side by its own rules — retail at MRP over the counter, distribution with party rates, schemes and credit.

If your distribution business runs on retail software plus spreadsheets for schemes and outstanding, that's usually the clearest sign it's time for a system built for the job.
