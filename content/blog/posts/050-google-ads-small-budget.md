Google Ads has a reputation among small businesses for burning money. Usually that's not because search ads don't work, but because the default settings are built for advertisers with large budgets. A small budget needs a narrow, disciplined setup.

Here's one that works for most local and service businesses.

## Before you spend anything

### Know what a customer is worth

Work out roughly what an enquiry is worth to you: average sale value, how many enquiries become customers, and your margin. If one in five enquiries becomes a ₹10,000 sale with 30% margin, an enquiry is worth up to about ₹600 in profit. That's your ceiling for cost per enquiry.

### Set up conversion tracking

Track the actions that matter: form submissions, calls from ads and from the website, WhatsApp clicks. Without tracking, you'll optimise for clicks — which is how budgets disappear without results.

### Have a page worth sending people to

Send ads to a specific page about the service, with a clear way to call, message or fill a short form. The home page is rarely the right destination.

## Campaign setup for a small budget

### Search only

Start with a Search campaign. Turn off the Display Network and search partners until you have data — they can spend budget on lower-intent traffic.

### Tight location targeting

Target only the areas you actually serve. Use the setting that targets people *in* or regularly in those locations, rather than people merely interested in them.

### Schedule ads for business hours

If enquiries need a quick callback, run ads when someone can answer. A call missed at 9 pm is money spent for nothing.

### Few, focused ad groups

Group keywords by service, with a separate ad and landing page for each. "AC repair" and "AC installation" are different searches with different intent.

## Choosing keywords

Pick searches that show buying intent:

- service + location: "AC repair Jayanagar"
- service + intent: "GST billing software price"
- urgent needs: "emergency plumber near me"

Avoid broad, informational searches like "how to fix AC" — they bring clicks from people who want to do it themselves.

Start with **phrase match** and **exact match** to control what triggers ads.

### Negative keywords

Add negatives from day one and every week after:

- *free, jobs, course, training, salary, DIY, how to*
- competitor names, unless you mean to target them
- services you don't offer

Check the **search terms report** weekly. It shows exactly what people typed, and is where most savings come from.

## Write ads that filter as well as attract

- Put the service and location in the headline.
- Mention what makes you different: same-day service, years of experience, warranty.
- Include price guidance if you can — it filters out people looking for something much cheaper.
- Add assets: call buttons, location, sitelinks to key services.

## Bidding

With little conversion data, start with **manual CPC** or **maximise clicks with a bid cap**, so you control cost per click. Once you have steady conversions, test **maximise conversions** or a **target cost per acquisition**.

## Weekly housekeeping

1. Read the search terms report; add negatives.
2. Pause keywords with spend but no conversions over a meaningful period.
3. Check cost per enquiry against your ceiling.
4. Listen to a few call recordings or review form leads for quality.
5. Test one new ad variation at a time.

## Typical mistakes

- Sending traffic to the home page
- No conversion tracking, or tracking page views instead of enquiries
- Targeting the whole city when you serve three neighbourhoods
- Never checking search terms
- Changing everything at once, so nothing can be learned

A small budget can't afford waste, but it can be profitable. Narrow targeting, intent-rich keywords, a focused landing page and steady weekly attention will beat a larger budget run on default settings.
