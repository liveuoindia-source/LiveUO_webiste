WhatsApp messages get read. That's exactly why they're so easy to ruin. A few irrelevant promotions, sent too often, and customers block the number — taking away a channel that could have brought repeat business for years.

Done well, WhatsApp marketing feels like service. Done badly, it feels like spam.

## Build the list with consent

Customers should choose to receive marketing messages from you on WhatsApp. Good places to ask:

- **at checkout**, with an unticked box: "Send me offers and updates on WhatsApp"
- **at the counter**, with a QR code that opens a chat
- **on your website**, via a click-to-chat button and a clear message about what they'll receive
- **in ads**, with click-to-WhatsApp campaigns where the user starts the conversation

Tell people what they're signing up for and roughly how often. Record when and how each person opted in.

## Send what customers value

Ask: would the customer be glad to receive this?

Usually yes:

- back-in-stock alerts for something they asked about
- reorder reminders for products they buy regularly
- early access to a sale they'd shop anyway
- useful information related to their purchase — care tips, usage guides
- appointment or service reminders

Usually no:

- generic "Happy Monday!" greetings
- forwarded images with no context
- daily offers on products they've never shown interest in

## Segment, even simply

You don't need complex tools to send more relevant messages. Start with a few segments:

- customers who bought in the last 30 days vs those who haven't for six months
- product categories they've bought
- location, for store events or local offers

A message to 500 interested people outperforms one to 5,000 indifferent ones — and gets far fewer blocks.

## Frequency and timing

- Fewer, better messages. A few a month is a sensible starting point for most businesses.
- Send during reasonable hours — not early morning or late at night.
- Avoid stacking marketing messages on top of order updates.

Watch your block and opt-out rates after every campaign. They tell you faster than anything when you're overdoing it.

## Write messages that work on a phone

- Lead with the point in the first line.
- Keep it short — a few lines.
- Include one clear action: a link, a button, or "reply YES".
- Use an image only if it adds information.
- Make opting out easy: "Reply STOP to unsubscribe".

## App vs Platform

The **WhatsApp Business app** has broadcast lists that only reach people who have saved your number — fine for very small businesses.

The **WhatsApp Business Platform** (via a provider) supports approved templates, segmentation, buttons, analytics and connection to your store or CRM. It's what you need once your list grows or you want automated, triggered messages.

## Measure what matters

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Delivered and read | Whether messages reach people |
| Click or reply rate | Whether the content is relevant |
| Orders or bookings | Whether it earns money |
| Block and opt-out rate | Whether you're overdoing it |
| Cost per order | Whether it's worth the spend |

## The rule of thumb

Treat a customer's WhatsApp like their front door. They let you in because they expected something useful. Bring that, and they'll keep opening it. Abuse it, and the door closes — usually for good.
