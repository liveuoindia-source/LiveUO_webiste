Pharmacy demand follows the weather. The first heavy rains bring a rush for anti-diarrhoeals and ORS; winter brings cough and cold; summer brings sunscreen and rehydration. A pharmacy that's prepared catches that demand. One that isn't sends customers elsewhere — and then sits on unsold stock when the season ends.

## Learn from last year

Your own sales history is the best guide. Pull category and item sales for the same months last year and look for:

- **when demand started rising** — the week, not just the month
- **how high it peaked** compared to normal
- **when it fell back**
- **which items** led the increase
- **where you ran out**, and for how long

Local patterns often differ from what distributors predict.

## Build a seasonal calendar

| Season | Typical categories | Plan from |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-monsoon and monsoon | Anti-diarrhoeals, ORS, antifungals, repellents | Weeks before the usual onset |
| Winter | Cough and cold, anti-allergy, moisturisers | Before temperatures drop |
| Summer | Sunscreen, rehydration, prickly heat, cooling products | Before peak heat |
| Festival periods | Antacids, digestives, general OTC | Before major festivals |

Adjust the categories and timing to your area and customers.

## Adjust reorder levels before the season

Raise minimum and maximum levels for seasonal items a few weeks before demand rises, based on last year's peak. Lower them again as the season ends. Seasonal items with fixed year-round levels either run out in season or overstock off season.

See [setting reorder levels](/resources/blog/pharmacy-reorder-levels-min-max/) for the method.

## Buy in stages

Rather than one large purchase at the start:

1. **First order** before demand rises, covering early weeks.
2. **Top-up orders** weekly, based on actual sales.
3. **Stop buying** as demand starts to fall.

Staged buying reduces leftover stock if the season is mild or late.

## Watch the season as it unfolds

Compare this year's weekly sales with last year's for seasonal categories. If demand is running ahead, increase top-ups. If it's lagging, slow purchases. A weekly ten-minute check prevents both stockouts and overbuying.

## Plan the end of the season

As demand falls:

- stop reordering seasonal items
- move excess stock to branches where demand continues
- return unexpired surplus where distributors allow
- note what was left over, to buy less next year

## Talk to distributors early

Seasonal products can be short across the market at peak demand. Agree quantities and delivery schedules with distributors before the season, and ask about schemes — but check scheme quantities against your expected sales first.

## Keep chronic medicines stable

Seasonal planning shouldn't distract from the essentials. Medicines for chronic conditions — diabetes, blood pressure, thyroid — have steady demand from regular patients who depend on you. Never let them run short because seasonal stock took up the budget.

## After each season

Spend fifteen minutes reviewing:

- items that ran out, and when
- items left over, and how much
- whether timing matched last year

Each season's notes make the next one easier. Over two or three years, seasonal planning becomes routine — and the pharmacy is known as the one that always has what people need when they need it.
