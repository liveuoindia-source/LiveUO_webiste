For many distributors and manufacturers, orders still arrive by phone call, WhatsApp message or a rep's notebook. Someone retypes each one into the billing system. Errors creep in, orders get missed, and customers call again to ask where their stock is.

A B2B ordering portal lets business customers place orders themselves — at any hour, with their own prices, and with full visibility of status.

## What makes B2B different

| | Consumer store | B2B portal |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Anonymous, many | Known, registered |
| Prices | Same for all | Customer-specific |
| Payment | Upfront | Often on credit |
| Orders | Occasional | Frequent, repeat |
| Quantities | Small | Cases, minimum quantities |
| Key feature | Discovery | Speed of reordering |

## Essential features

### Customer-specific pricing

Each customer sees their own price list, discounts and schemes when they log in.

### Quick reorder

Most B2B orders repeat. Let customers reorder from past orders, or from a saved list, in a few taps.

### Stock availability

Show what's available, so customers don't order items you can't supply.

### Credit limits

Show outstanding balance and credit available. Block or flag orders above the limit, according to your policy.

### Minimum quantities and pack sizes

Enforce case quantities and minimum order values.

### Schemes

Apply volume schemes and free quantities automatically, and show customers what they qualify for.

### Order tracking

Status from received to dispatched to delivered, with invoice and delivery details.

### Mobile first

Many retailers will order from a phone. The portal — or an app — must work well on one.

## Connect to your back office

The portal should be connected to your billing or ERP system:

- orders flow in for approval, invoicing and dispatch
- stock, prices and schemes flow out to the portal
- outstanding balances update as payments are received

Without integration, the portal just becomes another inbox someone has to retype from.

## Getting customers to use it

- start with your most regular customers
- show them how quickly they can reorder
- have reps help them place the first few orders
- keep phone ordering available during the transition
- offer a small incentive for portal orders, if appropriate

## What you gain

- fewer order-entry errors
- orders received around the clock
- reps spending time selling rather than taking orders
- better data on what each customer buys
- faster invoicing and dispatch

## Start focused

A first version with login, customer prices, quick reorder, credit visibility and order status covers most of the value. Add schemes, analytics and apps once customers are using it.

For many distributors, a B2B portal is the most practical form of e-commerce: not selling to strangers, but making it effortless for existing customers to buy more.
