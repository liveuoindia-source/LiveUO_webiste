Most billing errors and almost all billing fraud share one thing: a transaction was changed after the fact, and nobody can tell who changed it or why. An audit trail makes those changes visible.

## What an audit trail records

For each important action:

- **who** — the user
- **what** — the transaction or record
- **when** — date and time
- **the change** — old value and new value
- **where** — device or location, if available

Important actions include:

- creating, editing, cancelling and deleting invoices
- changing rates, discounts and taxes on bills
- payment entries and changes
- changes to item, customer and price masters
- stock adjustments
- user and permission changes

## Why it matters

### Prevents quiet fraud

Common patterns — a bill edited down after the customer paid, a cancelled bill whose cash was kept, a discount added afterwards — become easy to spot when every change is logged with a name.

### Resolves disputes

When a customer disputes an invoice, the audit trail shows exactly what was billed and whether anything changed.

### Supports compliance

For companies, Indian rules require accounting software to maintain an audit trail that cannot be disabled, and auditors report on it. Other businesses benefit from the same discipline. Check with your accountant what applies to you.

### Protects honest staff

When a shortage appears, the audit trail shows what actually happened, rather than leaving everyone under suspicion.

## What a good audit trail looks like

- **always on** — cannot be disabled by users
- **tamper-proof** — users cannot edit or delete log entries
- **complete** — covers transactions and masters
- **searchable** — by user, date, document and type of change
- **retained** for the period you need

## Review exceptions, not everything

Nobody reads every log entry. Set up a weekly exception review:

| Exception | Why look |
|---|---|
| Invoices cancelled after payment | Possible cash diversion |
| Invoices edited after printing | Possible manipulation |
| Large manual discounts | Margin leak |
| Price or tax changes on masters | Errors affect every future bill |
| Stock adjustments without reason | Hidden losses |
| Activity outside business hours | Unusual access |

Most weeks, there'll be nothing alarming. The fact that someone looks changes behaviour.

## Pair it with permissions

An audit trail tells you what happened; permissions limit what can happen. Use both:

- restrict editing after printing
- require approval and reasons for cancellations
- limit master changes to a few people

## Questions to ask your software vendor

- Can the audit trail be switched off?
- Can any user, including admin, edit or delete it?
- Does it record old and new values?
- Does it cover master data as well as transactions?
- How long is it kept?
- Can I export it for an auditor?

An audit trail is invisible on a good day and invaluable on a bad one. Make sure yours is always on, complete, and occasionally read.
