Marketing dashboards are full of numbers: impressions, reach, clicks, CTR, engagement. Most of them describe activity. A handful describe whether marketing is making money. Those are the ones that should drive decisions.

## Cost per lead (CPL)

**What it is:** marketing spend divided by the number of leads — enquiries, form fills, calls.

**Example:** ₹20,000 on ads, 80 enquiries → CPL of ₹250.

**Use it to:** compare channels and campaigns on how cheaply they generate interest.

**Watch out for:** cheap leads that never buy. CPL alone rewards quantity, not quality.

## Conversion rate

**What it is:** the share of leads that become customers.

**Example:** 80 leads, 12 customers → 15%.

**Use it to:** judge lead quality by source, and sales follow-up.

## Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

**What it is:** total cost to win one new customer — marketing spend, and ideally sales costs — divided by new customers.

**Example:** ₹20,000 marketing → 12 customers → CAC of about ₹1,670.

**Use it to:** decide what you can afford to spend to grow.

## Return on ad spend (ROAS)

**What it is:** revenue attributed to ads divided by ad spend.

**Example:** ₹20,000 spend, ₹1,00,000 revenue → ROAS of 5.

**Use it to:** compare campaigns that sell directly, especially in e-commerce.

**Watch out for:** ROAS uses revenue, not profit. A ROAS of 5 on a product with 15% margin loses money; on a product with 50% margin it's healthy. Calculate your break-even ROAS: 1 divided by gross margin. At 25% margin, break-even ROAS is 4.

## Customer lifetime value (CLV)

**What it is:** the profit a customer brings over the whole relationship.

**Simple calculation:** average order value × orders per customer over a period × gross margin.

**Example:** ₹1,500 per order × 6 orders a year × 30% margin → about ₹2,700 a year.

**Use it to:** set how much you can afford to pay to acquire a customer. If CLV is ₹2,700 and CAC is ₹1,670, acquisition is profitable over a year, even if the first order isn't.

## Putting them together

| Question | Metric |
|---|---|
| Which channel brings enquiries cheaply? | CPL |
| Which channel brings good enquiries? | Conversion rate |
| What does a customer cost us? | CAC |
| Is this campaign profitable on the first sale? | ROAS vs break-even ROAS |
| Can we afford to spend more? | CLV vs CAC |

## Make the numbers trustworthy

- **track conversions properly** — forms, calls, WhatsApp, purchases
- **record lead source** for every enquiry
- **link leads to sales** in your CRM or billing system
- **include all costs** — ad spend, agency fees, tools

Without this, every metric is an estimate.

## Activity metrics still have a place

Impressions, reach and click-through rate help diagnose problems — an ad nobody clicks, a page nobody reads. Use them to improve campaigns, not to judge whether marketing works.

## A monthly review

1. spend, leads and CPL by channel
2. conversion rate by channel
3. CAC by channel
4. ROAS for direct-sales campaigns
5. CLV trend for customers acquired in previous months

Decisions follow naturally: move budget towards channels with the best CAC relative to CLV, and fix or stop the rest.

Marketing that's measured on profit gets better every month. Marketing measured on likes rarely does.
