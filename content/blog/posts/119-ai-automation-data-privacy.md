Automation and AI often run on personal data: customer names and phone numbers, purchase histories, employee attendance, patient prescriptions, support conversations. Used well, that data makes service faster and better. Handled carelessly, it becomes a legal and reputational risk.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets obligations for businesses that process digital personal data. Whatever your size, it's worth building privacy into automation from the start.

## Start with purpose

For each automation, write down:

- what personal data it uses
- why — the specific purpose
- the basis for using it — usually consent, or another ground the law allows
- how long it will be kept

If you can't explain the purpose simply, the automation probably uses more data than it needs.

## Collect and use only what's needed

- a delivery update needs a phone number and order status — not date of birth
- a sales forecast needs sales by product — not customer names
- a chatbot answering FAQs doesn't need account access

Remove, mask or aggregate personal details wherever the task doesn't require them.

## Consent and notice

Where you rely on consent:

- tell people clearly what you'll do with their data
- ask separately for marketing messages
- make withdrawing consent easy, and honour it
- keep a record of consent

## Check AI and automation vendors

Before sending personal data to any tool or service, ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Where is data stored and processed? | Location and access |
| Is our data used to train their models? | Your data could inform outputs for others |
| Who at the vendor can access it? | Insider risk |
| How long is it retained? | Data should not outlive its purpose |
| How is it secured? | Encryption, access controls |
| What happens on a breach? | Notification and support |

Put data protection terms in the contract.

## Control access internally

- role-based access — staff see only what their job needs
- individual logins and audit trails
- remove access when people leave
- secure storage of credentials and API keys

## Sensitive data deserves extra care

Health information, financial details and data about children need particular caution. Pharmacies, clinics, finance and education businesses should be especially careful about what goes into automation and AI tools.

## Keep people in the loop

For decisions that significantly affect individuals — credit, employment, pricing, health — keep a person reviewing automated outputs, and be able to explain how a decision was made.

## Plan for requests and incidents

- be able to find, correct or delete a person's data on request
- know who handles privacy requests and grievances
- have a plan for data breaches, including notification where required

## A practical checklist

1. List the personal data each automation uses.
2. Remove what isn't needed.
3. Confirm consent or other basis.
4. Review vendors and contracts.
5. Set access controls and logging.
6. Define retention and deletion.
7. Assign a person responsible for privacy.

Privacy isn't a barrier to automation. Built in from the start, it makes automation safer, earns customer trust, and avoids costly rework when obligations are tested.
