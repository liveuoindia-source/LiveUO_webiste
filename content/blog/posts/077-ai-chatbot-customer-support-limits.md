AI chatbots have improved dramatically. They understand natural language, reply in several languages, and can answer questions at any hour. That makes them genuinely useful for support — and also makes their failures more convincing. A chatbot that confidently gives a customer the wrong return policy is worse than no chatbot at all.

## Where chatbots do well

- **Repetitive questions** — hours, location, pricing, policies, documents required
- **Order and booking status**, when connected securely to your system
- **Collecting details** before a person takes over — order number, issue, photos
- **Guided steps** — how to reset a password, set up a product, book an appointment
- **After-hours coverage** for simple queries
- **Multiple languages**, for businesses serving diverse customers

These share a pattern: the answer exists, it's stable, and it's the same for everyone.

## Where they fail

- **Missing information.** Asked something outside its knowledge, a chatbot may guess.
- **Policy edge cases.** "Can I return this if…?" often needs judgment.
- **Emotional customers.** An upset customer wants to be heard by a person.
- **Complex problems** spanning several systems or departments.
- **Anything with legal, medical or financial consequences.**

## Design it to fail safely

### Limit what it knows

Give the chatbot approved content — FAQs, policies, product information — and instruct it to answer only from that. If it doesn't know, it should say so and offer a person.

### Make hand-over easy

"Talk to a person" should always be available. Hand over with the conversation history so the customer doesn't repeat themselves. Outside working hours, take details and promise a callback time.

### Trigger hand-over automatically

Hand over when the customer:

- asks for a person
- repeats the same question
- uses words that signal frustration or a complaint
- raises refunds, cancellations or anything sensitive

### Protect data

Only connect to the systems needed, with proper authentication. Don't let the chatbot reveal account information without verifying the customer. Handle personal data in line with India's data protection law.

## Launch carefully

1. **Start with your top 20 questions.** They usually cover a large share of volume.
2. **Test with real conversations** from your inbox before going live.
3. **Review transcripts weekly** — wrong answers, missed hand-overs, new questions.
4. **Update content** as policies change.

## Measure it

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Resolved without a person | Main efficiency gain |
| Hand-over rate | Too high means poor coverage; too low may mean customers are stuck |
| Satisfaction after chat | Are customers happy with the outcome? |
| Accuracy on sampled chats | Is it telling the truth? |
| Support tickets for simple questions | Should fall |

## The right expectation

A support chatbot is best thought of as a fast, tireless first-line assistant: it answers the easy questions instantly and prepares the hard ones for your team. Built that way, customers get faster answers and your team spends its time where people are actually needed.
