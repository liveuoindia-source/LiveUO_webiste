Insulin, many vaccines, some eye drops, biologics and certain injectables need refrigeration from the manufacturer to the patient. A pharmacy is one link in that chain, and a weak one if storage and records aren't taken seriously. A medicine stored at the wrong temperature may look normal and still not work properly.

## Receiving cold-chain stock

When a delivery arrives:

1. **Check it first.** Cold-chain items should be unpacked before anything else.
2. **Check the packaging** — cold box, ice packs or gel packs still cold, temperature indicator if provided.
3. **Check the label conditions** and expiry.
4. **Move to the fridge immediately**, before recording the purchase.
5. **Refuse or flag** anything that arrived warm, and inform the distributor the same day.

## Storage

- **Use a dedicated pharmaceutical refrigerator** where possible, not a domestic fridge used for food.
- **Don't overfill** — air needs to circulate.
- **Keep products away from the walls and the cooling element**, where freezing can happen.
- **Don't store in the door**, where temperature fluctuates most.
- **Keep products in original packaging.**
- **Organise by expiry**, so first-expiry stock is used first.

## Temperature monitoring

- use a **minimum-maximum thermometer** or a **digital data logger**
- record the current, minimum and maximum temperature at least twice a day
- reset the min-max after each reading
- keep the log, signed by the person recording

A data logger with alerts is better: it records continuously and can notify someone when the temperature goes out of range, including at night.

## Power cuts

Plan for them:

- know how long your fridge holds temperature with the door closed
- keep the door shut during outages
- connect the fridge to backup power if available
- record outages and temperatures afterwards

## When the temperature goes out of range

1. **Don't panic, and don't sell.** Quarantine the affected stock in the fridge, clearly labelled.
2. **Record** the time, duration and temperature reached.
3. **Contact the manufacturer or distributor** with these details. Many can advise whether the product is still usable based on their stability data.
4. **Fix the cause** — door left open, faulty fridge, overloading.
5. **Dispose of affected stock properly** if advised, and record it.

## Dispensing to patients

Cold chain doesn't end at the counter:

- dispense cold-chain items last, just before the patient leaves
- pack with a cold pack or insulated bag where appropriate
- tell the patient how to store it at home and how long it can be out of the fridge, per the label
- mark cold-chain items in your software so the counter is reminded every time

## Records to keep

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Temperature log | Shows storage was controlled |
| Delivery condition notes | Supports claims against distributors |
| Excursion reports | Shows what happened and what was done |
| Fridge maintenance | Shows equipment was looked after |
| Disposal records | Accounts for destroyed stock |

## Make it a routine

Assign responsibility per shift, place the log next to the fridge, and review it weekly. Cold-chain discipline is simple to follow and serious to neglect — it protects patients, protects the pharmacy, and shows inspectors and customers that you take quality seriously.
