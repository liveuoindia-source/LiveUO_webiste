Cash on delivery is how a large share of Indian shoppers still prefer to buy online. It lowers the trust barrier for a store they have never heard of, and for many stores it is the difference between a first order and no order.

It is also expensive in ways that don't show up on the order screen. A COD order that is refused at the door costs you shipping both ways, ties up stock for a week or more, and delays the cash from every order that does get delivered.

The goal for most stores is not to kill COD. It is to know what COD really costs, and to move the customers who would happily pay online.

## The real cost of a COD order

Look beyond the courier's COD charge. A realistic cost includes:

- **The COD handling fee** charged by the courier or aggregator.
- **Return to origin (RTO).** Refused or undeliverable orders, where you pay forward and return shipping and get nothing.
- **Unsellable returns.** Products that come back opened, damaged or past their season.
- **Cash flow delay.** COD remittance arrives days after delivery, while prepaid money is with you before dispatch.
- **Fake and impulse orders.** Orders placed with no real intent to buy, which almost always arrive as COD.

When stores calculate this honestly, the difference between a delivered prepaid order and a COD order is often larger than their entire margin on low-value products.

## Measure the right numbers first

Before changing anything, track these per payment method:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Orders placed | The headline number most stores watch |
| Orders delivered | What actually earns money |
| RTO rate | The share of shipped orders that come back |
| Average order value | COD and prepaid orders often differ |
| Cost per delivered order | Shipping, fees and returns, all in |

A change that cuts placed orders slightly but raises delivered orders and lowers RTO is a win, even if the first dashboard looks worse.

## Levers that move customers to prepaid

### Make prepaid cheaper or better

A small discount on prepaid orders, free shipping for prepaid only, or a free sample with prepaid are the most common nudges. Customers respond to a visible, immediate benefit. Show it next to the payment options, not hidden in the terms.

### Make paying online effortless

Many customers choose COD because the online payment step feels risky or tedious. Offer UPI prominently — it's the method most Indian shoppers trust — along with cards, net banking and wallets. Keep the payment step on a fast, familiar page, and make retrying a failed payment easy. A payment failure that pushes the customer to COD, or out of the checkout entirely, is a lost prepaid order.

### Add a small COD fee

A modest, clearly shown fee for COD is one of the strongest levers. Most genuine buyers accept it or switch to prepaid; it mostly discourages the orders you didn't want.

### Build trust before the payment step

Customers pick COD when they doubt the store. Clear return and refund policies, real contact details, delivery estimates, and visible reviews all reduce that doubt. So does an order confirmation that arrives instantly, by email and WhatsApp.

## Restrict COD where it performs worst

You don't have to treat every order the same. Common rules:

- **Cap COD by order value.** High-value COD orders carry the most risk.
- **Disable COD for pin codes** with persistently high RTO.
- **Limit COD for repeat refusers.** A customer who refused two parcels should be offered prepaid only.
- **Confirm high-risk COD orders** by call or WhatsApp before dispatch. A one-tap confirmation catches many impulse orders.

These rules need an order system that can apply them automatically at checkout, using your own delivery history. That's where a store built around your operations earns its cost — the rules are specific to your products and regions.

## When COD is still worth it

Keep COD where it brings customers you can't otherwise reach: first-time buyers, smaller towns, and categories where touching the product matters. For many stores, the right answer is COD as a first-order option, with every later order nudged towards prepaid as trust builds.

## Run it as an experiment

Change one lever at a time and measure delivered orders, RTO and cost per delivered order over a few weeks — long enough for returns to come back. Then keep what works.

The stores that handle COD well aren't the ones that removed it. They're the ones that know exactly what it costs them, and price, restrict and nudge accordingly.
